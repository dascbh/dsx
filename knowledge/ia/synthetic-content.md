# Synthetic content: labeling, provenance and detection

> **When to consult**
> - When displaying in the interface text, images, audio, video, code, summaries or classifications generated or altered by AI.
> - When designing image upload, export, sharing or publishing (what to preserve, what to remove).
> - When designing or describing a feature that "checks whether an image is AI".
> - When writing the conclusion of an origin check.
>
> **Label component:** the visual and placement rules are in [`label-ai-content`](../../patterns/ai/label-ai-content.md). This document covers the reasoning behind it and the file/provenance layer.

## 1. Labeling in the interface: principles

The label states **origin**; it does not prove accuracy or review. It works together with sources ([`ai-sources`](../../patterns/ai/ai-sources.md)), uncertainty ([`ai-uncertainty`](../../patterns/ai/ai-uncertainty.md)) and review ([`review-ai-output`](../../patterns/ai/review-ai-output.md)).

Essential decisions:
- **IF** the AI generated or **materially** transformed the content **THEN** label it at the point where the person interprets or uses that content.
- **IF** only part of the set came from the AI **THEN** the label delimits exactly that part.
- **IF** the assistance was minimal (spelling) **THEN** assess the context before labeling; do not equate it with generated content.
- **IF** there was no confirmed human review **THEN** never use "verified" or "reviewed".
- **IF** the content can be copied, exported or published **THEN** the origin information travels with it (in the label and, for files, in the metadata/credentials of section 2).
- A textual label with an accessible name; never just an icon, sparkle or color. Some jurisdictions impose specific transparency obligations; check the regulatory context.

Vocabulary faithful to what happened:

| Situation | Text |
|---|---|
| Generated from scratch | "Generated by AI" |
| The person's content rewritten or completed | "Edited with AI" / "Completed with AI" |
| Summary of sources | "AI-generated summary" + access to the sources |
| Reviewed by a person, with a record | "Generated by AI · reviewed by [role] on [date]" |

## 2. Provenance in the file: the basics

For images, there are different layers of information. They are not different names for the same thing.

| Layer | Function | Field examples | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EXIF** | Technical capture data | Make/model, date, exposure, aperture, ISO, orientation, GPS | The date depends on the device clock; editable; camera data can survive AI edits |
| **IPTC** | Editorial description and rights | Creator, credit, caption, rights, license; recent versions of the standard include properties about AI use (system, prompt) | It is a declaration, not authorization or proof; presence of the AI fields depends on the tool |
| **XMP** | Structure for writing and carrying metadata between applications | Title, description, keywords, edit history; often carries the IPTC fields | Seeing "IPTC" and "XMP" together does not mean duplicated or conflicting data |
| **ICC profile** | Color interpretation | Embedded sRGB profile | Says nothing about authorship or AI use; an EXIF "color space: sRGB" field does not prove an embedded ICC profile |
| **Content credentials (C2PA)** | Provenance history with verifiable integrity | Issuer, declared actions (capture, edit, generation), validation status | Records **declared** origin; does not verify whether the depicted scene is true |
| **Embedded watermark** | A signal inserted into the content itself by compatible systems | Does not appear in the metadata list | Only a verifier compatible with that technology detects it |

Rules for whoever designs image flows:
- **Preserve the original** before any cleanup or editing.
- **Check before removing:** stripping metadata first destroys the history you wanted to understand.
- **Removing EXIF does not remove everything:** IPTC, XMP, ICC and watermarks follow their own mechanisms. Inspect the **resulting** file; do not trust the button's name.
- **Privacy by default in public copies:** offer to remove GPS from personal photos before publishing; archives may need to keep authorship and technical data.
- **When generating images with AI in the product**, write the provenance (C2PA credential and/or IPTC/XMP AI fields) and do not erase it on export.
- A thumbnail, screenshot or converted file is **another file**; it may have lost everything.

## 3. Limits of detection

Three families of technical signal, which are not equivalent:
1. **Metadata** (EXIF/IPTC/XMP): clues about software and workflow.
2. **C2PA credentials:** declared history with verifiable integrity.
3. **Embedded watermark:** presence of a signal from a specific system.

And two kinds of weak indication:
- **Classification detectors** return an estimate from their own method; without knowing the tests, the evaluated classes and the limits, a percentage is not proof.
- **Visual clues** (hands, shadows, text, reflections) help decide what to investigate, not to conclude. Human images have errors; generated images can be coherent.

### Interpreting results
| Finding | Permitted conclusion |
|---|---|
| A valid credential declares AI generation | Technical evidence about that step and that file; record issuer, action and status. The depicted facts remain to be investigated |
| A credential declares AI editing | "Edited with AI" ≠ "generated by AI"; do not extend the conclusion to the whole image |
| A software field names an AI tool | The file went through it (if the field was not altered); it does not measure how much was generated |
| No metadata, credential or watermark detected | **Inconclusive origin.** Compatible with a photo that lost its data and with an image generated without a record |
| Conflicting signals or validation failed | Record each observation; look for the original; do not pick the convenient signal |

**Golden rule:** "not detected" describes the reach of a test; it does not confirm absence. No single test identifies all content produced or edited by any AI.

### Writing the conclusion
Conclude with short, verifiable sentences, never "real" or "fake":
- "The analyzed file contains a valid credential that declares AI editing."
- "No compatible signals were found in the tests performed; the origin remains inconclusive."

### Five-step verification
1. Get the version closest to the original and record where it came from and where it has been.
2. Read the metadata; do not treat absence as proof.
3. Check content credentials; read the declared actions and the validation status.
4. Use a specific verifier when there is a reason, knowing what type of signal it looks for.
5. Compare with context: original publication, statement from the person responsible, earlier versions, independent sources.

## 4. Implications for verification UI

- Show **which type of signal** each check looks for and its scope.
- Use three states, not two: **signal found**, **signal not found (inconclusive)**, **check failed**.
- Never display an "Authentic" badge based on the absence of AI signals.
- When showing a detector percentage, state that it is an estimate and which method was used.
- Separate "verified provenance" from "true content".

## 5. Anti-patterns

- An AI label applied to the whole page because AI exists somewhere else on it.
- A label that is only an icon or sparkle.
- "Verified" without real review.
- Stripping metadata before checking.
- Concluding "not AI" because no signal was found.
- Treating an ICC profile or the absence of EXIF as an AI clue.
- Exporting an image generated in the product without provenance.
- A detector percentage presented as proof.

## 6. Checklist

- [ ] All generated or materially transformed content has a textual label, at the exact scope.
- [ ] The vocabulary distinguishes generated, edited, summarized and reviewed.
- [ ] Export and sharing preserve the origin information.
- [ ] Images generated in the product receive provenance (C2PA and/or IPTC/XMP).
- [ ] Upload flows preserve the original and check before cleaning.
- [ ] Metadata removal is confirmed on the resulting file; GPS removable in public copies.
- [ ] Verification UI has an "inconclusive" state and does not issue an authenticity badge based on absence.
- [ ] Conclusions written as verifiable observations, not "real/fake".
